What am I looking at?

The Minor Planet Center keeps a public list of newly spotted objects that might be near-Earth asteroids but are not yet confirmed. NASA/JPL's Scout system fits a rough orbit to each one as new observations come in. Those orbits are very uncertain while an object has only a few observations, and its position gets harder to predict with every passing day. That is why follow-up observations matter: if nobody measures an object again soon, it can be lost before anyone learns what it is.

This page ranks Scout's current list by an illustrative priority score: how much each object seems to need another look before its position becomes too uncertain to find. The score blends five factors, and the sliders let you change their weights. It is a demonstration of transparent triage logic. It is not an official method, and it says nothing definite about any object.
